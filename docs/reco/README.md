# My Wave — offline evaluation

`evaluation.md` in this folder is generated by `make reco-eval`
(`services/reco-service/cmd/reco-eval -seeds 5`). This page explains how to read it.
Architecture: [ADR 0010](../adr/0010-reco-service-my-wave.md).

## Why synthetic users

The dev installation has two users and a few dozen events, so any metric on real data would
be noise. `internal/eval` therefore simulates a world with known ground truth:

- **720 tracks** by 90 artists in 8 genres. Each track has a latent "sound" vector. Its
  31-dim audio descriptor is a random linear projection of that vector plus noise, plus a
  per-track **recording-level offset** (the real-world nuisance of hot masters vs quiet
  78 rpm transfers). 8 % of the tracks are brand new: no interactions before the split.
- **300 users**, each with genre preferences, a preferred sound (latent vector), a few
  favourite artists and a personal popularity bias. The true utility of (user, track)
  combines these terms.
- **70 events per user** from a listen process: complete, skip early or late, like, add to
  playlist, replay. Probabilities follow the utility, timestamps span 60 days, and the
  events go through the same `profile` weights and decay as production.
- **Time split:** the last 25 % of each user's positive events is the holdout. Recommenders
  see only the training part (catalog popularity is also computed from training plays).

## Metrics

- **Offline:** P@10, R@10, NDCG@10 and HitRate@10 over unseen holdout items.
  - **Coverage:** share of the catalog recommended to anyone.
  - **ILD:** mean pairwise audio distance inside a list.
  - **Artist diversity:** unique artists / list length.
  - **Novelty:** mean self-information of popularity, in bits.
  - **Cold-start recall:** recall restricted to new tracks.
- **Closed loop:** three wave batches of 10 per user. Simulated reactions (like / skip /
  neutral, drawn from the true utility) are fed back as session feedback exactly as
  music-service does. We measure like rate, skip rate, mean true utility, same artist back
  to back and repeats.
- Everything is averaged over 5 independent worlds (seeds 7–11). Weights were tuned by
  random search on a **different** world (`-tune N` uses seed + 1000) to avoid fitting the
  test worlds.

## Results (mean of 5 worlds)

| | Heuristic (ADR 0009 §6) | reco: full blend | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| NDCG@10 | 0.0828 | 0.0979 | **+18 %** |
| P@10 / R@10 | 0.0559 / 0.0836 | 0.0746 / 0.1123 | **+33 % / +34 %** |
| HitRate@10 | 0.449 | 0.532 | +18 % |
| Catalog coverage | 0.403 | 0.776 | ×1.9 |
| Cold-start recall | 0 | 0.188 | new tracks now surface |
| Novelty (bits) | 4.84 | 6.37 | less popularity-bound |
| Session like rate | 0.623 | 0.660 | +6 % |
| Session skip rate | 0.130 | 0.073 | **−44 %** |
| Same artist back-to-back | 1.5 % | 0 | hard constraint |

Popularity-only for scale: NDCG 0.053, session skip rate 0.71.

### Reading the ablations

- **CF (co-occurrence)** is the main source of "because you liked X". Without it NDCG drops
  to 0.096 and cold-start recall falls from 0.19 to 0.12: fewer good seeds for the
  content stand-in.
- **ALS ("similar listeners")** adds accuracy (0.096 → 0.098) and coverage (0.735 →
  0.776). Without ALS, cold-start recall *rises* (0.37): the new tracks' content stand-in
  wins more slots when nothing competes, at the cost of accuracy on the rest. The blend
  keeps ALS at 0.8.
- **Audio content** is close to neutral for warm items in the simulator, since offline
  accuracy is within noise. It is the reason new tracks surface (cold-start recall 0.146 →
  0.188) and it keeps sessions coherent (skip rate 0.074 → 0.073, utility 1.909 → 1.917).
  In production it also drives the modes and the "mood you like" explanations.
- **Taste profile** matters most in sessions: without it the like rate drops to 0.650 and
  utility to 1.905.
- **MMR** trades a little offline accuracy for diversity. Without it, artist diversity
  falls from 0.70 to 0.58 and audio ILD from 0.35 to 0.31.
- **Exploration** (ε + Thompson) costs about 1 % NDCG offline and buys coverage (0.73 →
  0.78) and cold-start recall (0.135 → 0.188). The same trade-off at production scale is
  what keeps the catalog from collapsing onto the head.

The heuristic's ILD and artist diversity are higher than reco's because it spreads over
popular tracks with no notion of taste coherence: "diverse but random". Reco's lists are
more coherent in sound and still never repeat an artist back to back.

## Caveats

- These are **simulated** users. The model of taste (genre + sound + artists + novelty) is
  plausible but it is our own. Treat the deltas as evidence that each layer does what it
  should, not as a forecast of production lift.
- With real traffic, measure online: like and skip rate by `strategy` (reco vs fallback)
  and by source (`src`, logged per served track), then A/B the weights through
  `RECO_WEIGHTS`.
- The real catalog is small (132 CC0 / public-domain recordings). Content neighbours are
  timbral: a solo-piano nocturne sits near solo-piano rags. Genre and style come from the
  profile and CF once there are listeners.

## Reproduce

```bash
make reco-eval                                   # 5 seeds → docs/reco/evaluation.md (~40 s)
cd services/reco-service
go run ./cmd/reco-eval -seeds 1 -users 100       # quick run
go run ./cmd/reco-eval -tune 40                  # random search of ranking weights (world seed+1000)
```
